## Introduction

Tom DeLonge occupies an unusual place in modern UFO culture because his influence comes less from claiming to have personally witnessed extraordinary events than from helping move military UAP (unidentified anomalous phenomena) material into mainstream public discussion. As the co-founder of [To The Stars Academy]({{ 'to-the-stars/' | relative_url }}) (TTSA), the former Blink-182 guitarist and singer became associated with the publication of U.S. military videos and with a network of former defence and intelligence officials. That combination gave his [advocacy]({{ 'advocacy/' | relative_url }}) an unusual level of public visibility.


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/ufos_and_celebrities_846269_celebrity_ufo_roundu_802bba_delonge_unidentified_147a20-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="De Longe Leap illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/ufos_and_celebrities_846269_celebrity_ufo_roundu_802bba_delonge_unidentified_147a20-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/ufos_and_celebrities_846269_celebrity_ufo_roundu_802bba_delonge_unidentified_147a20-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
The key issue, however, is not whether DeLonge raised awareness of UAP. He clearly did. The more important question is how public discussion often moved from "officially unidentified" to "therefore probably alien". That leap is not one made by the U.S. government, NASA or the Pentagon's current UAP investigations. Understanding where DeLonge's advocacy overlaps with documented events—and where it extends into broader extraterrestrial interpretations—is essential to understanding how celebrity influence can flatten different levels of evidence. ## What DeLonge helped bring into public view

DeLonge's greatest historical impact was organisational rather than evidential. In 2017 he launched To The Stars Academy, bringing together former [military]({{ 'military/' | relative_url }}) and intelligence figures including individuals with experience in government UAP programmes. Later that year, *The New York Times* published its landmark story about the Pentagon's Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP), alongside military cockpit videos that had been distributed through TTSA.

Those events changed the public conversation in several important ways:

* military UAP footage reached a much wider audience;
* discussion shifted from fringe UFO culture to mainstream national-security [reporting]({{ 'reporting/' | relative_url }});
* former government officials began speaking publicly through a celebrity-backed organisation rather than remaining largely unknown outside specialist circles.

Subsequent Navy statements confirmed that the released videos were authentic military recordings and that the objects shown remained officially "unidentified". Later, in 2020, the Department of Defense formally released the same videos. Those actions validated the authenticity of the recordings—not any particular explanation for what the objects were.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/navy-confirms-ufos-in-videos-posted-by-blink-182-singer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: syfy.com">[SYFY+2Global News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">syfy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Navy confirms UFOs in videos posted by Blink-182 singer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Navy confirms UFOs in videos posted by Blink-182 singer...September 18, 2019 — 18 Sept 2019 — UFO videos posted by Blink-182 singer...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 18, 2019</span></span></span>

This distinction is often lost in popular retellings. "The videos are genuine" and "the videos depict extraterrestrial craft" are separate propositions requiring different evidence.

## Where official confirmation stopped

The boundary between official findings and DeLonge's broader narrative is where evidence flattening most often occurs.

Official agencies have consistently drawn a careful distinction:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* the videos are authentic military recordings;
* some recorded objects remain unidentified because available data are insufficient;
* unidentified does not imply extraterrestrial origin.

</div>

NASA's UAP programme explicitly states that it has found no evidence that UAP represent alien technology while encouraging better scientific data collection. Likewise, the Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) has repeatedly reported that its investigations have found no verified evidence of extraterrestrial technology or hidden government programmes possessing alien craft. DeLonge, by contrast, has frequently argued in interviews and public appearances that non-human intelligence is deeply involved with the phenomenon and that governments possess far more knowledge than they publicly acknowledge. ## Why media narratives filled the gap

DeLonge's celebrity status amplified a communication pattern that often appears in UFO reporting.

A typical sequence runs as follows:


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/ufos_and_celebrities_846269_celebrity_ufo_roundu_802bba_delonge_unidentified_147a20-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="De Longe Leap illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/ufos_and_celebrities_846269_celebrity_ufo_roundu_802bba_delonge_unidentified_147a20-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/ufos_and_celebrities_846269_celebrity_ufo_roundu_802bba_delonge_unidentified_147a20-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. military footage is released;
2. officials describe an object as unidentified;
3. headlines emphasise official confirmation;
4. audiences infer that confirmation applies to alien explanations;
5. later clarifications receive less attention than the original story.

</div>

This process is especially powerful because "unidentified" sounds like a partial confirmation rather than a statement about incomplete information.

Entertainment media sometimes reinforced this dynamic. Stories highlighting that "the Navy confirmed Tom DeLonge's UFO videos" were technically accurate if referring to the authenticity of the footage and the use of the term "unidentified". Yet readers could easily come away believing the military had confirmed extraterrestrial craft, which it had not.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/navy-confirms-ufos-in-videos-posted-by-blink-182-singer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: syfy.com">[SYFY+2The FADER]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">syfy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Navy confirms UFOs in videos posted by Blink-182 singer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Navy confirms UFOs in videos posted by Blink-182 singer...September 18, 2019 — 18 Sept 2019 — UFO videos posted by Blink-182 singer...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 18, 2019</span></span></span>

The result is what might be called evidential compression: multiple distinct claims become compressed into one simplified narrative.

Instead of preserving separate questions—


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Were the videos authentic?<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/navy-confirms-ufos-in-videos-posted-by-blink-182-singer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: syfy.com">[syfy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">syfy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Navy confirms UFOs in videos posted by Blink-182 singer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Navy confirms UFOs in videos posted by Blink-182 singer...September 18, 2019 — 18 Sept 2019 — UFO videos posted by Blink-182 singer...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 18, 2019</span></span></span>
* Were the objects identified?
* Were they foreign technology?
* Were they sensor artefacts?
* Were they extraterrestrial?<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[media.defense.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 18 Mar 2024 — • No evidence of extraterrestrial origin of UFO/UAP were discovered...</span></span></span>

—the discussion often collapsed into a single conclusion that "the government admitted UFOs are real", with "UFO" itself implicitly meaning alien spacecraft.

## Why DeLonge became uniquely influential

Many celebrities express belief in extraterrestrial life, but DeLonge differed in several respects.

First, he invested years building relationships with former defence and intelligence personnel rather than limiting himself to interviews or [documentaries]({{ 'documentaries/' | relative_url }}).

Second, he became associated with documents, videos and personnel connected to genuine government UAP programmes rather than relying only on personal testimony.

Third, his longstanding public interest in UFOs pre-dated the 2017 surge in official attention, allowing later events to appear as validation of at least part of his campaign.

These genuine achievements also strengthened public confidence in his broader claims, even though those claims extended well beyond what official investigations concluded. That illustrates a common cognitive shortcut: when one aspect of a person's work is validated, audiences often assume neighbouring claims have received similar confirmation.
