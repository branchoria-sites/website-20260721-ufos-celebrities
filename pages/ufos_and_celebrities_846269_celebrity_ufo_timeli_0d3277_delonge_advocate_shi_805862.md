## Introduction

Tom DeLonge's place in the modern UFO story is unusual because he did not remain simply a celebrity expressing unconventional beliefs. Instead, the former Blink-182 guitarist attempted to reposition himself as an organiser, media entrepreneur and intermediary between entertainment audiences, former government officials and the public. That shift became especially significant after 2017, when renewed attention to unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP) coincided with official disclosures, major newspaper [reporting]({{ 'reporting/' | relative_url }}) and growing political interest in the subject. Whether one accepts DeLonge's own interpretations or not, his career illustrates a new model of celebrity influence: using fame not merely to popularise an idea, but to assemble institutions, attract experienced personnel and shape how a controversial topic entered mainstream discussion.

## From musician identity to UFO organisation

For much of his music career, DeLonge's fascination with UFOs was treated as an eccentric personal interest. Blink-182's 1999 song *[Aliens Exist]({{ 'aliens-exist/' | relative_url }})* became one of the best-known pop-cultural expressions of UFO enthusiasm, reinforcing his image as a musician intrigued by extraterrestrial themes rather than someone attempting public [advocacy]({{ 'advocacy/' | relative_url }}). His departure from Blink-182 in 2015 marked a turning point, as he increasingly devoted his time to projects centred on UFO research, storytelling and public engagement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/tv-and-radio/2020/sep/15/star-of-bethlehem-spaceship-tom-delonges-new-career-ufo-expert-blink-182" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Guardian&#x27;People need to open their minds!&#x27; – Tom De Longe on his</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">He talks about spooked moon-walkers, a mysterious 1940s crash – and what Donald Trump and Boris Johnson dare...Read more...</span></span></span>

The key institutional step came in 2017 with the launch of **[To The Stars Academy]({{ 'to-the-stars/' | relative_url }}) of Arts & Science (TTSA)**. Rather than presenting itself as a traditional UFO organisation, TTSA combined several functions:

* entertainment production through books, films and television;
* public discussion of UAP investigations;
* scientific and aerospace ambitions;
* collaboration with former [military]({{ 'military/' | relative_url }}) and intelligence personnel.

This hybrid structure reflected DeLonge's belief that popular culture could attract audiences who would not normally follow defence or aerospace issues. It also distinguished his approach from earlier celebrity involvement, which had usually stopped at interviews, memoirs or personal testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710274/000110465920072815/tm2022367d1_partii.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC+2Rolling Stone]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">to the stars academy of arts and science inc.In UNIDENTIFIED, Elizondo is speaking out for the first time with Tom DeLonge, co-founder...</span></span></span>

An important part of the strategy was narrative continuity. DeLonge continued producing fiction such as the *Sekret Machines* series while simultaneously promoting non-fiction discussions of UAP. Instead of treating entertainment and advocacy as separate activities, he used each to reinforce interest in the other, creating an audience that could move between novels, documentaries, interviews and public events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/tv-and-radio/2020/sep/15/star-of-bethlehem-spaceship-tom-delonges-new-career-ufo-expert-blink-182" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Guardian&#x27;People need to open their minds!&#x27; – Tom De Longe on his</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">He talks about spooked moon-walkers, a mysterious 1940s crash – and what Donald Trump and Boris Johnson dare...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Why former officials changed the tone

Perhaps the most consequential aspect of DeLonge's transition was not his celebrity status itself but his ability to recruit individuals with recognised backgrounds in government and defence.

TTSA's leadership included figures such as Luis Elizondo, formerly associated with the Pentagon's Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP); Christopher Mellon, a former Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence; Steve Justice, formerly of Lockheed Martin's Skunk Works; and scientist Hal Puthoff. Their participation altered public perception because discussions increasingly featured people whose previous careers were associated with national security rather than entertainment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710274/000110465920072815/tm2022367d1_partii.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC+2Pitchfork]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">to the stars academy of arts and science inc.In UNIDENTIFIED, Elizondo is speaking out for the first time with Tom DeLonge, co-founder...</span></span></span>

This did not validate every claim made by participants. Critics continued to question interpretations of evidence, the evidential [standards]({{ 'standards/' | relative_url }}) being applied and the degree to which media attention outpaced available data. Nevertheless, the composition of the organisation shifted the conversation from "a rock musician believes in UFOs" towards "former defence and intelligence officials are discussing unidentified aerial phenomena in public". That distinction proved important in changing how journalists framed subsequent reporting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/what-is-up-with-those-pentagon-ufo-videos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">what is up with those pentagon ufo videos</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The article included videos of UAPs and featured accounts from Navy pilots. The timing also mattered. TTSA emerged immediately before *The New York Times* revealed the existence of AATIP in December 2017. Because TTSA had already established relationships with several of the people involved, DeLonge's organisation became part of the infrastructure through which many early public interviews, television appearances and discussions were organised.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.newsweek.com/pentagon-released-ufo-footage-tom-delonge-1500607" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newsweek.com">[Newsweek]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newsweek.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pentagon released ufo footage tom delonge 1500607</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academy of Arts &amp; Sciences—an organization set up DeLonge to investigate UFO sightings—in late 2017...Read more...</span></span></span>

## How entertainment attention helped UAP coverage

DeLonge's greatest practical contribution was arguably not producing new scientific evidence but acting as a bridge between audiences that rarely overlapped.

Entertainment media provided access to millions of people familiar with Blink-182 but unfamiliar with military aviation or defence bureaucracy. Through documentaries, podcasts, books and History Channel programming, UAP discussions reached viewers who might never have read congressional documents or specialist aerospace reporting. The television series *Unidentified: Inside America's UFO Investigation* exemplified this approach by combining former officials, pilot testimony and documentary storytelling in a format designed for mainstream audiences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710274/000110465920072815/tm2022367d1_partii.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC+2Yahoo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">to the stars academy of arts and science inc.In UNIDENTIFIED, Elizondo is speaking out for the first time with Tom DeLonge, co-founder...</span></span></span>

The publication of Navy videos through TTSA, followed by broader reporting and later official confirmation that the footage depicted unidentified aerial phenomena rather than fabricated material, further increased DeLonge's visibility. Importantly, official acknowledgement concerned the authenticity of the videos as genuine military recordings, not confirmation of extraterrestrial origins. That distinction is frequently lost in public discussion but remains central to understanding the evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.newsweek.com/pentagon-released-ufo-footage-tom-delonge-1500607" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newsweek.com">[Newsweek+2VICE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newsweek.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pentagon released ufo footage tom delonge 1500607</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academy of Arts &amp; Sciences—an organization set up DeLonge to investigate UFO sightings—in late 2017...Read more...</span></span></span>

Media coverage consequently shifted its emphasis. Instead of focusing on celebrity belief alone, journalists increasingly examined questions such as:

* how governments investigated UAP;

## The strengths and limits of DeLonge's advocacy model

DeLonge's approach demonstrated several strengths.

His public profile attracted attention that specialised researchers could rarely generate on their own. His entertainment background also helped package technically complex subjects into accessible documentaries, interviews and books. Moreover, by encouraging participation from former officials, he contributed to a noticeable change in tone from speculation about extraterrestrials towards discussion of aviation safety, intelligence assessment and government reporting practices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esquire.com/entertainment/music/a37532754/tom-delonge-interview-2021/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esquire.com">[Esquire]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esquire.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Vindication of Tom De Longe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">About life, about music, and—because he is, to my knowledge, the only rock star to have co-founded a company ...Read more</span></span></span>

## Why his career marks a new stage in celebrity UFO history

Earlier celebrity UFO stories generally revolved around personal sightings, beliefs or rumours that became part of a public figure's mythology. DeLonge represents a different phase. Rather than offering another celebrity testimony, he attempted to build an organisational ecosystem linking popular culture, publishing, television, former officials and public advocacy.

That shift helps explain why he occupies a distinctive place in the [timeline]({{ 'timeline/' | relative_url }}) from John Lennon's 1970s sighting to the modern UAP era. His significance is not primarily that he became a famous believer, but that he used celebrity status to create a platform through which institutional voices, media production and public debate increasingly intersected. Regardless of how future evidence reshapes the UAP question itself, that transformation in the role of celebrity has become one of the defining features of the post-2017 UAP landscape.
