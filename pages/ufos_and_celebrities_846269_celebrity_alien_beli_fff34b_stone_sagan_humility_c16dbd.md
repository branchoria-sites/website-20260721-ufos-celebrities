## Introduction

Emma Stone's public comments about aliens stand out because they were not presented as evidence for UFOs or extraterrestrial visitation. Instead, while promoting *Bugonia* at the 2025 Venice Film Festival, she framed belief in extraterrestrial life as an exercise in humility, explicitly invoking astronomer and science communicator Carl Sagan. ## How Stone framed belief through Sagan

At the Venice press conference for *Bugonia*, Stone was asked about extraterrestrial life while discussing a film whose plot revolves around a woman suspected of being an alien. Rather than appealing to mystery or personal experience, she cited Carl Sagan as one of her intellectual influences.

Stone explained that she admired Sagan through his television series *Cosmos*, saying she had fallen in love with both his scientific outlook and his [philosophy]({{ 'philosophy/' | relative_url }}). She paraphrased what she understood to be his central idea: believing that humans are the only intelligent life in the vast universe is "a pretty narcissistic thing" to think. The structure of her argument matters:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* It begins with the size and age of the universe rather than with unexplained sightings.
* It appeals to probability and perspective rather than certainty.
* It treats extraterrestrial life as compatible with scientific curiosity rather than as proof of extraordinary claims.

This echoes Sagan's long-standing emphasis on "cosmic perspective": the idea that recognising humanity's small place in the universe encourages intellectual humility rather than human exceptionalism.

## Why this is not evidence of alien visitation

Entertainment [headlines]({{ 'headlines/' | relative_url }}) often compress statements like Stone's into "Emma Stone believes in aliens." That wording is accurate only in a limited sense and can obscure what she actually argued.

Stone did **not** claim:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* to have witnessed a UFO;
* to possess evidence of extraterrestrials visiting Earth;
* that unidentified aerial phenomena have an alien explanation.

</div>

Instead, she expressed the view that intelligent life elsewhere in the universe is plausible because of the universe's immense scale. That position is shared by many scientists who study astronomy and astrobiology while remaining cautious about claims that UFO reports demonstrate alien visitation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://variety.com/2025/film/festivals/emma-stone-believes-in-aliens-bugonia-venice-press-conference-1236500554/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: variety.com">[Variety]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">variety.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">emma stone believes in aliens bugonia venice press conference 1236500554</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Emma Stone Jokes That She Believes in Aliens at &#x27;Bugonia...28 Aug 2025 — Emma Stone Jokes That She Believes in Aliens at &#x27;Bugonia...</span></span></span>

This distinction is important within celebrity discussions of UFOs. A philosophical belief that life probably exists elsewhere occupies a very different evidential category from eyewitness testimony or claims about government concealment of alien spacecraft.

## Cosmic humility rather than human exceptionalism

Stone's comments are notable because they shift the discussion away from sensational questions—"Have aliens visited us?"—towards a broader question about humanity's place in the cosmos.

The underlying philosophy can be summarised as follows:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* **The universe is enormous.** Modern astronomy has revealed billions of galaxies containing enormous numbers of stars and planets.
* **Humans should be cautious about assuming uniqueness.** Stone's use of the word "narcissistic" reflects the idea that placing humanity at the centre of creation without evidence risks intellectual overconfidence.
* **Uncertainty is acceptable.** Believing alien life is likely is compatible with acknowledging that we currently lack proof of contact with extraterrestrial civilisations.

</div>

This perspective is consistent with the tradition of scientific humility associated with Sagan: remaining open to possibilities while insisting that extraordinary claims require convincing evidence.

## Why Stone's comments fit the broader celebrity philosophy category

Among celebrity discussions of extraterrestrial life, Stone's remarks are unusual because they rely on an explicitly scientific and philosophical framework rather than anecdote.

Her argument belongs to a long-running tradition of cosmic humility: recognising that the observable universe is so vast that assuming Earth hosts the only intelligent life may be an expression of human self-importance rather than a conclusion supported by evidence.
